Watch your land come back to life

Tupureel is a free, community-powered photopoint monitoring service. Groups set up camera stations at key spots in their landscape, community members contribute photos by email, and Tupureel automatically weaves those repeat photographs into timelapses that reveal the slow, hopeful story of regeneration.

How it works

Three simple steps — no specialist equipment needed.

1

Set up a photopoint station

Choose a spot in your landscape — a hillside, a waterway, a revegetation planting. Mark the exact camera position so every future photo is taken from the same point. A fixed post, a notch in a fence, or a painted rock all work well.

2

Community members email photos

Anyone visiting the site takes a photo from the marked position and emails it to your station's unique address. No app, no account, no upload portal required — just an ordinary email with the photo attached.

3

Tupureel generates your timelapse

Tupureel aligns, sorts, and assembles your contributed photos into a timelapse video automatically. Watch seasons change, plantings establish, waterways clear, and wildlife return — one frame at a time.

About Tupureel

Tupureel began as a small experiment: could the humble, shareable photograph become a tool for community science and landscape stewardship? Repeat photography — taking a photo from exactly the same spot, over and over, across months and years — is one of the most powerful and accessible ways to document ecological change. Tupureel makes that practice easy for any community group, anywhere in Aotearoa New Zealand.

The service is entirely free to use. There is no subscription, no advertising, and no data sold. The code that runs Tupureel is open to anyone to read, fork, improve, and deploy.
